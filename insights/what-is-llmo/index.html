What is LLMO, and why it matters now

How AI assistants decide which businesses to name, and what you can do about it.

Short answer: LLMO (Large Language Model Optimization) is making your business easy for AI assistants to understand, verify and recommend. Think of it as SEO for answers instead of links.

Why this matters

More and more people skip the search results and ask an assistant directly: "Who's a good immigration lawyer in Glendale?" or "Where can I buy upholstery fabric in LA?" The assistant gives one short answer that names a few businesses. If you're not in it, you're invisible for that customer.

How an AI model decides who to name

Models build answers from what they learned in training plus, increasingly, sources they retrieve live. In both cases they favor businesses that are:

  • Clear: it's obvious what you do, for whom, and where.
  • Consistent: your name, services and location match everywhere they appear.
  • Corroborated: other trusted sites, listings and reviews confirm you.
  • Quotable: your pages contain short, direct answers the model can lift.

LLMO vs. SEO vs. GEO vs. AEO

SEO optimizes for ranked search results. AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) targets direct answers like featured snippets and voice answers. GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) and LLMO both target AI-generated answers. In practice, the work overlaps heavily, and the best strategy does all of it from one foundation of clear, trustworthy content. See the glossary for more terms.

Five things you can do this month

  1. Write one sentence that says exactly what you do, for whom, and where, and put it on your homepage.
  2. Add structured data (schema.org) for your business and services.
  3. Publish an FAQ that answers the questions customers really ask, in two or three sentences each.
  4. Make your name, address, phone and services identical on Google, Yelp, LinkedIn and industry directories.
  5. Add an llms.txt file summarizing your business for AI systems.
